# Module: Asks (Cialdini, Influence)

Load for emails that ask someone to do something they do not have to do: review a doc, lend an engineer, share data, approve budget, make an intro, take on a task.

## Why this module exists

Most asks fail because they are vague, effortful, or give the reader no reason to say yes now. Cialdini's seven principles of persuasion (reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity) describe why people say yes. Used honestly, they make a fair ask easy to accept. Used as tricks, they damage trust with colleagues you will email again next week, so every lever below must be true.

## Pick one or two levers, never all seven

Choose the levers that are genuinely true for this ask. One well-placed lever reads as context; four read as a sales pitch.

| Principle | Honest email move | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | Give something first or show what they get back | "I have attached the churn cut you asked for last week. In return, could you..." |
| Commitment and consistency | Link the ask to something they already said or agreed | "At Monday's review you said accuracy is the Q2 priority. This review is the fastest way to protect it." |
| Social proof | Name others already doing it or on board | "Dana and Tomás have already signed off on their sections." |
| Authority | Cite the data or the expert behind the ask | "The Q1 survey shows action item accuracy as the top churn driver (06-user-feedback)." |
| Liking | Real common ground or genuine credit, one line | "Your Slack v2 auth work is the model I am copying here." |
| Scarcity | The real cost of waiting, never invented urgency | "The vendor holds this price until Fri 20-03." |
| Unity | The shared goal or team identity | "This gets both our teams to the June Enterprise GA." |

Scarcity and urgency must be real. A fake deadline gets found out and every later deadline is discounted.

## Make the ask easy to accept

- **One specific ask.** "Review sections 2 and 4" beats "take a look".
- **Effort stated.** "About 15 minutes" or "two days of one engineer".
- **Deadline with the reason.** "By Fri 20-03, so I can share it with engineering Monday."
- **An easy out.** For a favour, a line like "If this week does not work, next Tuesday is fine" raises yes rates because it lowers the pressure.
- **Ask in the first two lines.** The supporting why comes after.

## Pass check

- [ ] The ask, effort, and deadline are all in the email, the ask in the first two lines.
- [ ] One or two levers used, each one true and checkable.
- [ ] No invented urgency, no flattery beyond one genuine line.
