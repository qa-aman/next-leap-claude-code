# Module: Announce to many people

Use when the email goes to a team, a company, or a customer base and has to be remembered
a week later: a launch, a process change, a reorg, a decision that affects how people
work. Framework: the Heath brothers' SUCCESs checklist from *Made to Stick*.

## SUCCESs, as email checks

| Trait | Question to ask the draft | Email move |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | Can the reader repeat the core message in one sentence? | Find the one sentence. It becomes the BLUF line. Everything else supports it. |
| Unexpected | Is there one fact that breaks what the reader assumed? | Lead the "Why it matters" with it. "Action items are wrong one time in three" beats "accuracy improvements". |
| Concrete | Can the reader picture it? | Name the button, the screen, the date, the number. "Starting Monday 23-03-2026 the summary shows a confidence badge next to each action item" not "improved transparency". |
| Credible | Why should they believe it? | One data point or one named source, not a list. |
| Emotional | Does the reader feel why this matters to them? | One sentence on what changes in their day. Not what changes for the company. |
| Stories | Is there a two-line example of a real person hitting the problem? | A single user quote or incident, with date and role, beats a paragraph of benefits. |

## Structure

1. BLUF: what is changing, for whom, from when.
2. Why it matters, bolded, with the unexpected fact and the reader's stake.
3. What changes for you, as two to four concrete numbered points.
4. What does not change, one line. Readers fill silence with worry.
5. Where to go deeper: the doc, the demo, the FAQ, the channel for questions.
6. Who to ask, by role.

## Announcement-specific rules

1. Role names, not personal names, in anything customer-facing.
2. One announcement per email. Two changes in one mail halves the recall of both.
3. The date format is DD-MM-YYYY, and every date has a day of the week when the change
   is within two weeks.
4. If the change takes something away, say so in the first screen. Burying it is found
   out and costs trust.

## Anti-patterns

1. "We are excited to announce". The reader's excitement is the goal, not yours.
2. Benefits as adjectives: faster, better, seamless. Use the number.
3. Fifteen bullets. If it needs fifteen, it needs a doc and the email is the summary.
