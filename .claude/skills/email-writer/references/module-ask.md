# Module: Ask for something

Use when the email's job is to get a yes: time, budget, headcount, a review, an intro,
a favour from another team. Framework: Cialdini's six principles of influence. These are
ethical levers, not tricks. Use the ones that are true for this ask and skip the rest.

## Before drafting

Write down: what exactly you want, by when, what it costs the reader, and what they get.
If you cannot name what they get, the ask is not ready.

## The six principles, as email moves

| Principle | What it means | Email move | Skip if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | People return what they receive | Open with something you did or can do for them, or attach the work already done so they only review | You have given nothing and cannot |
| Commitment | People act consistently with what they said before | Reference their own prior statement or goal: "You flagged churn as the Q2 priority, this is the fastest lever on it" | They never committed to anything related |
| Social proof | People follow peers | Name who else has agreed, reviewed, or is doing the same | Nobody has, do not fake it |
| Authority | People defer to credible sources | Cite the data, the customer quote, the expert, not your title | The evidence is thin |
| Liking | People say yes to people they like | Be warm, be specific about them, keep it short | Never skip, this is baseline courtesy |
| Scarcity | People act on real deadlines and limits | State the real deadline and what is lost after it | The deadline is invented |

## Structure

1. BLUF: the ask, the date, in one line.
2. Why it matters to them (not to you).
3. Two or three supporting points, each using one principle above, strongest first.
4. Make saying yes cheap: offer the smallest version of the ask, or say exactly what you
   need from them and how long it takes ("15 minutes to review the attached, reply
   yes or with edits").
5. Close with the next step and an exit: "If this is not the right week, tell me and I
   will reschedule."

## Anti-patterns

1. Leading with your constraint ("I am under pressure to...") instead of their gain.
2. Stacking all six principles. Two or three true ones beat six strained ones.
3. A fake deadline. Readers remember, and the next real one gets ignored.
4. Asking for "thoughts". Ask for a decision, a number, or a yes.
