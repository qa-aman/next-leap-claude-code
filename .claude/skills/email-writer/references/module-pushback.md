# Module: Say no, push back, renegotiate

Use when the email has to decline a request, challenge a deadline or scope, or change a
deal without damaging the relationship. Two frameworks: Voss for the sentences, Fisher and
Ury for the logic.

## Fisher and Ury: the logic of the pushback

1. **Separate the person from the problem.** Name the problem (the date, the scope), never
   the person's judgement.
2. **Interests, not positions.** Their position is "ship by 28-03". Their interest is
   probably "have something to show the board on 31-03". Find the interest and address it.
   A position can only be accepted or refused. An interest can be met five ways.
3. **Invent options for mutual gain.** Offer at least two alternatives that meet their
   interest: a smaller scope on the same date, the full scope on a later date, a demo
   build now and production later.
4. **Use objective criteria.** Anchor on a number, a precedent, or a standard, not on
   "we feel it is too tight". "The last three integrations each took 6 weeks" is a
   criterion.
5. **Know your BATNA** (best alternative to a negotiated agreement). Know what happens if
   they say no to every option, and make sure the email does not need their yes to leave
   you somewhere acceptable.

## Voss: the sentences that lower the temperature

1. **Label the emotion.** "It seems like this date matters because of the board review."
   "It sounds like the team is worried we are slipping again." A label lets the reader
   feel understood before you disagree. Use "it seems" or "it sounds like", not "I think
   you feel", which reads as a diagnosis.
2. **Calibrated questions.** Open questions that begin with "how" or "what" and hand the
   problem back without saying no. "How do we keep the board demo on 31-03 if the full
   scope needs six weeks?" "What would a version you could show on that date look like?"
   Never "why", which reads as an accusation in writing.
3. **The accusation audit.** If the reader is likely to think "here comes another delay",
   say it first: "You are probably expecting another slip here." Naming it drains it.
4. **"No" is the start, not the end.** A clear "we cannot do X by Y" followed by "here is
   what we can do" is stronger than a soft maybe that becomes a broken promise later.

## Structure

1. BLUF: the position, clearly. "We cannot ship the full Salesforce scope by 28-03-2026.
   Here are two ways to keep the board demo on 31-03."
2. Label: one sentence that shows you understand their interest.
3. The criterion: the number or precedent that makes the date unrealistic.
4. Two or three options, each meeting their interest, with the trade-off of each.
5. A calibrated question that invites them to choose or to add an option.
6. Close with the door open and the date by which you need their pick.

## Anti-patterns

1. Apologising in the first line. It signals the no is negotiable when it is not.
2. Explaining your workload. They have their own.
3. One option only. That is a position, and positions get refused.
4. Passive voice to avoid ownership ("it has been decided that").
