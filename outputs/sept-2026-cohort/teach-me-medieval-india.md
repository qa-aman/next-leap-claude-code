## Checklist

### Stage 1 - The problem (why the period exists, what forces were in play)
- [ ] 1.1 Why "medieval" India is dated c. 1200 (or c. 750) and what changed at each boundary
- [ ] 1.2 The post-Gupta fragmentation: the tripartite struggle (Pratiharas, Palas, Rashtrakutas) and why no one won
- [ ] 1.3 Why the northwest frontier kept being the entry point (Ghaznavid raids, Ghurid conquest, 1192 Tarain)
- [ ] 1.4 The branches available c. 1200: Rajput regional kingdoms, Turkic military state, southern polities (Cholas fading, Hoysalas, Kakatiyas)
- [ ] 1.5 The core structural problem every ruler faced: extracting land revenue from a huge agrarian base with thin loyal manpower

### Stage 2 - The solution (what was actually built, and why that way)
- [ ] 2.1 Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526): the five dynasties and what each one's "design decision" was (Iltutmish's iqta and the Forty, Balban's kingship, Alauddin's market control and revenue, Tughlaq experiments, Lodi decentralisation)
- [ ] 2.2 Iqta system: what it solved, what it broke, why it kept getting reformed
- [ ] 2.3 The south as the alternative: Vijayanagara (1336) and Bahmani (1347), nayankara system, why the Deccan became a separate chessboard
- [ ] 2.4 Mughal state (1526-1707): Babur's gunpowder edge at Panipat, Sher Shah's revenue template, Akbar's mansabdari + zabt + sulh-i-kul as one integrated design
- [ ] 2.5 Edge cases: Muhammad bin Tughlaq's token currency and Daulatabad, Aurangzeb's Deccan wars and jizya, the Maratha response (Shivaji, 1674)
- [ ] 2.6 Why the Mughal design worked for 150 years and then jammed (jagirdari crisis)

### Stage 3 - The context (why it matters downstream)
- [ ] 3.1 Cultural synthesis: Bhakti and Sufi movements, Indo-Persian art and architecture, birth of Urdu / Hindavi
- [ ] 3.2 What the British inherited: revenue records, the zamindar, the district, the idea of a Delhi-centred empire
- [ ] 3.3 The historiography fight: "Muslim rule" vs "Indian rule by Muslim dynasties", and why the framing matters today
- [ ] 3.4 Economic picture: India as a share of world GDP c. 1700 (UNVERIFIED number), the trade networks, and why decline is debated
